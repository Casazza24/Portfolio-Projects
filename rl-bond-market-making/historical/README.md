# Historical (pre-correction) results

Everything listed here comes from the original implementation (git commit
`abae89b`), before the correctness pass on branch `rl-objective-fixes`. It is
kept as a record of the project's development. **None of it should be read as
a validated result.** Current, reproducible results are in `../results/` and
`../figures/`, produced by the scripts in `../experiments/`.

## Why these results are superseded

1. **Different objectives.** The RL environment charged a running inventory
   penalty and a terminal penalty, while the finite-difference "ground truth"
   charged only the terminal penalty. The comparison in Section 1 compared
   solutions to two different problems.
2. **Volatility did not enter the benchmark.** `sigma` drove simulated prices
   but appeared nowhere in the FD recursion or the objective.
3. **The FD recursion was not exact for the simulator.** It dropped the
   probability that both sides fill in the same step (about 6% per step at
   typical quotes) and checked the ask-side inventory limit after the bid
   fill.
4. **The quoted closed form was not Avellaneda-Stoikov.** Under the corrected
   objective it scores about 30.5 against an optimum of about 66.2.
5. **Biased policy gradients.** Several actors evaluated the Gaussian
   log-density at the *clipped* action. Near the lower quote bound the
   resulting gradient estimate has the wrong sign (see `../tests/test_policy.py`).
6. **Multi-bond benchmark.** The multi-bond FD solver ignored correlation (so
   the problem it solved was separable) while still enumerating the full
   exponential grid; the environment's cross-inventory penalty used half the
   portfolio-variance weight.
7. **Evaluation on training reward.** Performance claims (for example the
   "about 60%" neural-network advantage in Section 3) compared average
   training rewards of stochastic exploring policies from single runs, not
   frozen policies on independent episodes across seeds.
